Core focus:
. DECT-2020 NR is a wireless communication standard designed for large-scale IoT networks with a large number of devices. In such networks, a sink node collects data from multiple sources. In this paper, we modeled a DECT-2020 sink node as a discrete packet arrival system, where data packets are processed in batches. We compared two types of systems with diﬀerent arrival disciplines, Early Arrival and Late Arrival, across several metrics, including the age of information (AoI), which measures the freshness of the received data. The results showed how the diﬀerence in the order of batch arrival aﬀected network perfor- mance and Age of Information. Additional context and findings:
The rapid development of ﬁfth-generation (5G) networks creates new opportu- nities for enabling applications that demand massive machine-type communica- tions (mMTC) and ultra-reliable low-latency communications (URLLC). These capabilities are of particular importance in the context of the Internet of Things (IoT), where millions of devices are expected to communicate simultaneously with high levels of reliability, resilience, and scalability. Among the promising technologies in this domain is the DECT-2020 New Radio (NR) protocol, standardized by the European Telecommunications Stan- dards Institute (ETSI) [1–4].
