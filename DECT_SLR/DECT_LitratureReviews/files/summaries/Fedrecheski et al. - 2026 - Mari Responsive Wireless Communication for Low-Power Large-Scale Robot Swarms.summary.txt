Additional context and findings:
Large-scale robotic swarms are the next frontier in distributed autonomous r systems. Swarms of micro-robots [1] could perform inspection and damage repair in hard-to-reach places, such as engines, building interiors, and satellites er in orbit. Their applications span from warehouse automation [2] to innovative user interfaces [3] and minimally invasive medicine [4]. Large swarms of small pe robots can also be useful to experiment with swarm behavior in more realistic scenarios before committing to full-scale robots, helping to close the simulation- reality gap that is common in swarm robotics. Communication is a major challenge to the advance of swarm systems [5]. This includes not only peer-to-peer (P2P) communication among close-by robots, ot but also communication with “control towers” used to control and deploy a swarm. That is, even if robots have a robust P2P communication system, they tn still need some communication link with a central entity to receive missions. This includes the user interface and the user experience involved in human- swarm interactions, where users can want to dynamically change the behavior of a swarm dynamically [6]. Moreover, a global communication link with swarms rin is needed to perform operational tasks, such as uploading firmware [7] and col- lecting logs. As a concrete use case, we are building the DotBot testbed, comprising ep 1,000 small wheeled robots designed for indoor environments, e.g. a building floor.
