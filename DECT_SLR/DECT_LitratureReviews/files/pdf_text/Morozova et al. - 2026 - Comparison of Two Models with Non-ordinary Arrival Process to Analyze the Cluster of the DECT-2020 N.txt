       Abstract. DECT-2020 NR is a wireless communication standard
       designed for large-scale IoT networks with a large number of devices.
       In such networks, a sink node collects data from multiple sources. In this
       paper, we modeled a DECT-2020 sink node as a discrete packet arrival
       system, where data packets are processed in batches. We compared two
       types of systems with diﬀerent arrival disciplines, Early Arrival and Late
       Arrival, across several metrics, including the age of information (AoI),
       which measures the freshness of the received data. The results showed
       how the diﬀerence in the order of batch arrival aﬀected network perfor-
       mance and Age of Information.


       Keywords: DECT-2020 · URLLC · mMTC · age of information ·
       queuing theory · discrete time Markov chain


1    Introduction

The rapid development of ﬁfth-generation (5G) networks creates new opportu-
nities for enabling applications that demand massive machine-type communica-
tions (mMTC) and ultra-reliable low-latency communications (URLLC). These
capabilities are of particular importance in the context of the Internet of Things
(IoT), where millions of devices are expected to communicate simultaneously
with high levels of reliability, resilience, and scalability.
    Among the promising technologies in this domain is the DECT-2020 New
Radio (NR) protocol, standardized by the European Telecommunications Stan-
dards Institute (ETSI) [1–4]. Unlike conventional cellular systems that rely on
centralized base stations and ﬁxed infrastructure, DECT-2020 NR operates in
the unlicensed 1880–1930 MHz frequency band and supports a fully decentralized
mesh network topology (see Fig. 1).

    In DECT-2020 NR, each radio device (RD) can act as a transmitter (RDPT ),

with densities of up to one million devices per square kilometer [5], which signif-
icantly exceeds the capacity of traditional wireless technologies such as Wi-Fi,
LTE-M, or NB-IoT.

    Another important characteristic of DECT-2020 NR is its half-duplex trans-
mission mode. In this case, a relay node (RDFT,PT ) alternates between receiving
packets from child nodes (RDPT or RDFT,PT ) and transmitting packets to the
parent node (RDFT,PT or RDFT ). Such an operation can be naturally repre-
sented by a queueing model with batch arrivals and batch services, where the
arrival batch size is variable while the service batch size remains ﬁxed.
    When modelling a tact-based systems such as DECT-2020 NR, it is impor-
tant to have a clear understanding of the sequence of actions in a slot, which
is especially important in half-duplex data transmission mode. Specialized liter-
ature distinguishes between two main disciplines: Early Arrival (EA) and Late
Arrival (LA) [6]. As the names suggest, in the ﬁrst case, a data packet is accepted
at the beginning of the slot and transmitted further through the system at the
end of the slot. In the second case, the data packet stored on the node is ﬁrst
transmitted through the system, and only at the end of the slot the new data
packet arrives. In addition to DECT-2020 NR, EA and LA can also be used in
IAB technology, which can also operate in half-duplex data transmission mode.
    This paper focuses on the modeling and performance evaluation of a DECT-
2020 NR network cluster using discrete-time queuing systems. Special attention
is given to the analysis of packet transmission delays in multi-hop routing sce-
narios and the inﬂuence of the stochastic nature of mMTC traﬃc on overall
network performance.
    This article proposes two mathematical models of the DECT-2020 NR clus-
ter. To this end, Sect. 2 constructs a mathematical model of packet transmission
by a relay node (RDFT,PT ) in the form of two diﬀerent queueing systems, tak-
ing into account the peculiarities of mMTC traﬃc, i.e. multiple random access.

Section 3 describes a numerical experiment illustrating the diﬀerence between
two mathematical models applied to a real DECT-2020 NR node.

Related Works. In recent years, discrete-time queues have been attracting much
attention, partly due to their practical relevance. Dividing time into slots and the
need to select diﬀerent arrival disciplines (EA or LA) allows for more accurate
modeling of telecommunication networks.
     Earlier, within the framework of DTMC studies, authors compared and ana-
lyzed models with diﬀerent arrival policies. In the article by Gupta and Goswami
[6], a discrete-time system with a ﬁnite buﬀer and bulk service was analyzed. The
authors considered two policies—AF (arrival ﬁrst) and DF (departure ﬁrst)—
showing that AF reduces delays and losses, while DF can be eﬀective under high
load. Similarly, the paper [7] examined the relationships between queue lengths
at diﬀerent epochs (arrival, departure, and random) for a discrete-time queue
with D-BMAP arrivals, also under diﬀerent arrival policies (EA and LA). The
authors used properties of the discrete-time batch Markovian arrival process
(D-BMAP) and probability generating function methods to establish exact rela-
tionships between distributions. The results allow characteristics at one epoch
to be expressed in terms of another, simplifying system analysis. The article
[8] by Goswami and Chaudhry presents the ﬁrst explicit analysis of the sojourn
time distribution for a discrete-time queue with batch service Geo/Geoa,b /1. The
authors propose a novel analytical approach, utilizing the roots of the character-
istic equation inside and outside the unit circle, to derive closed-form expressions
for the sojourn time probability generating function and its probability distribu-
tion. Research has also been conducted on systems with bulk arrivals and bulk
service. The article by Claeys [9] focused on delay analysis in the discrete-time
GeoX /Geoc /1 system with bulk arrivals and bulk service. The authors consid-
ered two service policies: IBSP (immediate-batch service policy) and FBSP (full-
batch service policy). Additionally, Chang and Choi [10] analyzed a discrete-time
system with a ﬁnite buﬀer where the server operates in bulk arrival and bulk
service mode and can also take vacations. Discrete-time systems are also actively
used in the study of Age of Information (AoI). For example, in the study [11]
the authors obtained the stationary distribution of AoI and derived analytical
expressions for the average age of information and other performance metrics
using a discrete-time queueing system.
     In contrast to the works presented above, the authors of this article analyze
a discrete-time system with batch arrivals of variable size and batch service of
ﬁxed size under EA and LA disciplines. For both systems, explicit formulas for
the probability generating function of the waiting time until the start of service
were derived.

    The DECT-2020 network cluster consists of D terminal devices RDPT with
one relay RDFT,PT as repeater node or the sink RDFT as the ﬁnal receiver node,
in which the age of information from the terminal device RDPT transmitted
in the uplink towards the base network is estimated. The system model of the
DECT-2020 network considered in this work is based on the previously published
study [12]. At speciﬁed by half duplex mode time intervals, each terminal device
generates a data packet and attempts to transmit it to the receiver via repeater,
where the packets await further transmission along the route. The number of
packets received by RDPT,FT node is a random variable (r.v.), the distribution
of which depends on the number D of terminal devices RDPT . The repeater
RDFT,PT also has speciﬁed time intervals to transmit packets up to the parent
node. The number of packets transmitted by node RDPT,FT during the trans-
mission time interval is also random, but in the most congested links, such as
the link from RDFT to the core network, it can be considered constant, and its
value is determined by the duration of the transmission interval.

2.1   Queueing System
We present the mathematical model of the RDFT,PT node as an inﬁnite buﬀer
discrete-time queue with batch arrivals of variable size and batch service of ﬁxed
size, formally denoted as Geo[1,A] |Geo[B] |1|∞. Packets arrive in batches of no
more than A packets, that is given by D, and are served in batch with ﬁxed size
B, which corresponds to the average number of packets transmitted per time
slot, depended on the channel data rate.
    Batches of packets arrive in the system with the probability a per slot and
no packets arrive with the complementary probability a = 1 − a. Probability a
represents a probability of generating a data packet by at least one RDPT from
D RDPT . Probability b means that the batch service is completed during a slot,
and the probability that no batch completes the service during a slot, equals
to b = 1 − b. Probability b allows us to model the successful completion of a
packet batch by node RDFT at the end of the transmission interval. These allow
modeling of the half-duplex communication mode.
    In this paper, we consider two types of discrete-time system: Late Arrival
(LA) and Early Arrival (EA) [6]. There is diﬀerence in the order of actions in a
slot, see Fig. 2:
- LA system: at the beginning of the slot a batch of packets completes service
and leaves the system, then, depending on the number of packets remaining in
the queue, the next batch of size B either begins service immediately or in one
of the next time slots once B packets have accumulated in the queue and at the
end of the slot new batch of packets arrives;
- EA system: at the beginning of the slot new batch of packets arrives, then
if a server is free depending on the number of packets remaining in the queue
immediately or in the next time slot the batch of B packets begins service and
at the end of the slot leaves the system.
    Next, we will introduce some necessary deﬁnition which are the same for the
both systems. After that, we will derive formulas for each systems, separately.

4   Conclusion

In this work, we investigated a model of a DECT-2020 NR network cluster using
a discrete-time system with diﬀerent arrival disciplines. Based on the derived
PGF of the waiting time, we also succeed to obtain an analytical expression for
the PGF of the PAoI. We compared the average peak age of information in the
DECT-2020 NR network cluster under the EA and LA disciplines. As the results
of experiments, it is recommended to rely on the LA system, which provides an
upper bound for the delay metrics, when calculating and designing a DECT-2020
NR network. In future work, we also plan to focus on the study of discrete-time
systems with batch service of variable batch size. Estimating the distribution of
the incoming batch length is also the goal of our further research.
