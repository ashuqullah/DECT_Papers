Bridging Light and Radio: Hybrid Network
Architectures for IoT in the 6G Era
                     1                1                                1                          1
Mohammad Khalili , Amila Perera , Juha Häkkinen, Marcos Katz , Konstantin Mikhaylov


1. Centre for Wireless Communications, University of Oulu


Abstract

The massive scale and dense deployments of future Internet of Things (IoT), particularly within the
6G framework, expose the critical limitations of traditional Radio Frequency (RF) systems, namely
spectral congestion, interference, and security vulnerabilities. Optical Wireless Communication
(OWC) oﬀers a compelling complement, providing vast, unlicensed spectrum, immunity to RF
interference, and enhanced physical-layer security. However, OWC's susceptibility to blockages
and limited mobility hinders its standalone deployment, as it cannot, on its own, meet the
stringent connectivity and mobility requirements of many IoT applications. This article explores
the convergence of optical and radio networks and proposes a ﬂexible, adaptable, and resilient
connectivity framework tailored for modern IoT ecosystems. We present a holistic system
architecture that integrates single-mode RF, single-mode OWC, and uniﬁed hybrid Access Points
(APs) to eﬃciently manage the diverse and often conﬂicting objectives of various IoT applications.
While these hybrid RF/OWC APs oﬀer signiﬁcant advantages in cost, eﬃciency, and coordinated
management, they also introduce a primary deployment challenge: the inherent coverage mismatch
between the two technologies. To precisely address this critical issue, we demonstrate a practical
solution supported by a coverage equivalence analysis to validate the proposed framework. This
analysis quantiﬁes the required OWC transmitter density to match a given RF range, oﬀering
tangible guidance for system planning.

communication, networking and broadcast technologies, hybrid network architecture, internet of
