                                      ABSTRACT
Digital Enhanced Cordless Telecommunications New Radio+ (DECT NR+) is a recently
introduced non-cellular radio access technology operating in the global license-exempt
1.9 GHz band, specifically designed for dense Internet of Things (IoT) deployments. Target-
ing key Fifth Generation (5G) communications such as massive Machine-Type Communi-
cations (mMTC) and Ultra-Reliable Low-Latency Communications (URLLC), it promises
high reliability, low latency, and extended range. However, limited empirical data exists
on its practical performance.
   This thesis presents a comprehensive experimental evaluation of DECT NR+ at the
physical layer (PHY) using Nordic Semiconductor’s nRF9161 development kits, DECT
Shell (DeSh), and custom firmware. The testbed characterizes four key performance
metrics: communication range, throughput, latency, and energy efficiency. Range tests
conducted in both indoor and outdoor environments revealed a maximum Line-of-Sight
(LoS) range of 730 meters outdoors with 80% Packet Delivery Ratio (PDR). Meanwhile at
short range, maximum measured throughput was 2.498 Mbit/s at Modulation and Coding
Scheme (MCS) 4 with 8 sub-slots, compared to the theoretical maximum of 3.36 Mbit/s.
Latency measurements showed a minimum Round-Trip Time (RTT) of 2.255 ms for a
12-byte packet. Energy per bit ranged from 0.26 to 2.6 𝜇𝐽 depending on the MCS level.
   Findings confirm that DECT NR+ offers significantly higher range and data rates than
typical Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) solutions but incurs higher energy costs, in both
transmitter and receiver modes. These results highlight DECT NR+’s suitability for low
duty-cycle, long-range, and latency-sensitive IoT applications, while identifying key areas
for modem-level energy optimization.

Keywords: DECT NR+, IoT, latency, energy efficiency, communication range, throughput,
nRF9161 .

                                  1    INTRODUCTION

                              1.1 Background and motivation

Digital Enhanced Cordless Telecommunications (DECT) is a communication standard main-
tained by the European Telecommunications Standards Institute (ETSI). Originally designed to
support fast roaming between networked base stations, DECT initially targeted wireless Local
Area Network (LAN) applications. However, its most popular use case became single-cell
cordless phones connected to traditional analog telephone lines. These cordless phones enabled
users to move freely without the constraint of cables, leading to widespread adoption in homes
and offices.
    Over time, DECT evolved into various forms, supporting different wireless Internet-of-
Things (IoT) applications such as wireless audio systems, baby monitors, and sensor networks,
all operating in the license-free 1880-1900 MHz spectrum. However, with the rise of mobile
phones and the decline in cordless phone usage, this DECT band has become increasingly
underutilized. Although other standards were introduced for this band, including those for
wide-band wireless audio transmission, and home automation, few have fully leveraged its
potential.
    To revitalize the DECT spectrum, ETSI released a new standard in 2020 known as DECT-
2020 New Radio (NR), featuring an updated Physical layer (PHY). This standard targets Ultra-
Reliable-Low-Latency Communication (URLLC) and massive Machine-Type Communications
(mMTC) use cases and supports both point-to-point and mesh topologies. The commercial
implementation of this protocol, known as DECT NR+, is currently available through Nordic
Semiconductor’s nRF91 series System-in-Package (SiP) devices, which integrate DECT NR+
alongside Long-Term Evolution (LTE) modems due to similarities in their operation. Notably,
DECT NR+ operates in a globally allocated, non-cellular DECT band.
    In today’s world, the number of wireless devices continues to grow rapidly, placing increased
demand on the radio spectrum. Modern wireless technologies must therefore support high
data rates, robust operation in dense environments, and minimal communication delays. DECT
NR+ aims to address these challenges by focusing primarily on providing reliable, low-latency
communication in environments with high device density. Specifically, it targets two of the three
key objectives of Fifth Generation (5G) NR, URLLC and mMTC rather than Enhanced Mobile
Broadband (eMBB).
    This research evaluates the real-world performance of DECT NR+, focusing on its PHY-layer
implementation using Nordic’s nRF9161 development kits. While upper-layer features such as
Media Access Control (MAC) level interference and collision handling are not explored in this
work, the study evaluates the peak capabilities of the PHY layer. Therefore, the presented results
reflect performance in idealized conditions and may differ from application-level performance
in full-stack implementations.


                                   1.2 Problem statement

DECT NR+ has only recently become available in the commercial domain through Nordic’s
nRF91 series SiPs. While several theoretical analyses of the DECT-2020 New Radio (NR)
standard exist, there is currently a lack of empirical data on the performance of DECT NR+ in
practical scenarios. To address this gap, this thesis presents a real-world performance evaluation
of DECT NR+ using the Nordic modem firmware and nRF9161 development kit.

                                   1.3 Research objectives

This study aims to address the following four objectives:
  • Evaluate the maximum communication range in both indoor and outdoor environments.
  • Determine the maximum achievable throughput under PHY constraints.
  • Measure the minimum latency using Round-Trip Time (RTT).
  • Analyze the energy efficiency, including energy per bit and Receiver (RX) mode power
      consumption.


                            1.4 Research scope and limitations

This research is limited to point-to-point communication between two DECT NR+ nodes. The
evaluation focuses exclusively on the PHY-layer and does not include MAC-layer or higher
layer functionalities. Features such as mesh networking and interference management are not
implemented. Custom firmware was developed for the experiments, which are designed to test
edge-case performance (i.e., best-case and worst-case scenarios) based on the relevant objective.
All measurements are specific to the Nordic nRF9161 development kit and the currently available
DECT NR+ modem firmware provided by Nordic Semiconductor.

This thesis is organized as follows:
  • Chapter 2 reviews selected related works and publications relevant to DECT-2020 NR
      standard, focusing on physical layer performance and experimental evaluations.
  • Chapter 3 provides technical background on the DECT-2020 NR standard and details the

  • Chapter 5 presents the results and discusses key findings related to performance metrics.
  • Chapter 6 concludes the thesis and suggests directions for future research.

                                  2   RELATED WORKS

Several research studies have evaluated the performance and potential of DECT-2020 NR com-
munication technology. This chapter summarizes key experimental and analytical findings from
recent publications that are relevant to the objectives of this thesis. Each section of this chapter
is dedicated to a single reviewed paper, outlining its methodology, key findings, and relevance
to this work. A summary of the reviewed studies is provided at the end of the chapter to
consolidate the main observations. As the available literature specifically on DECT NR+ is
extremely limited, the focus of this review is on studies related to DECT-2020 NR standard,
which forms the foundation of DECT NR+. Only papers that investigate PHY-layer performance
were considered. The reviewed works were primarily sourced from the Institute of Electrical
and Electronics Engineers (IEEE) Xplore digital library. Since the DECT-2020 NR standard
was introduced in 2020, all selected studies are relatively recent and reflect the current state of
the technology.


 2.1 DECT-2020 NR link distance performance in varying environments: models and
                                   measurements

Published in 2025 by Tampere University, this recent study [1] investigates the one-hop commu-
nication performance of DECT-2020 NR in both indoor and outdoor settings. The focus lies on
analyzing Received Signal Strength Indicator (RSSI) variation, path loss, Signal-to-Noise Ratio
(SNR), and packet success rate. These measurements are compared against theoretical models
such as Free Space Path Loss (FSPL), 3𝑟 𝑑 Generation Partnership Project (3GPP) Technical
Report (TR) 38.901, two-ray ground reflection, Okumura-Hata, and COST-231 Hata models.
   The experiments employed Nordic’s nRF9131 boards supporting DECT-2020 NR, equipped
with vertically polarized omnidirectional PulseLarsen W5095X antennas mounted 1.5 meters
above the ground. Transmitter (TX) power levels of -20 dBm, 0 dBm, and +19 dBm were tested
using channels 1667 and 1677 at 1899 MHz, and Modulation and Coding Scheme (MCS) levels
1 and 4. A packet length of four slots was used, with 300 samples per point at a 1 Hz rate.
Indoor distances were measured using a laser meter, while Global Positioning System (GPS) and
maps were used outdoors. Outdoor environments included football fields, forests, lake-sides,
sidewalks, power-line paths, and an observation tower around Tampere, Finland.
   Key findings indicate that a Line-Of-Sight (LOS) range of up to 190 m was achieved at
+8 dBm TX power in a factory corridor, maintaining over 90% packet delivery success. In Non-
Line-Of-Sight (NLOS) scenarios, ranges exceeding 60 m were recorded at 0 dBm TX power
through brick walls and cubicles. Reliable LOS communication was also demonstrated over
a 40 m corridor at -20 dBm TX power. Outdoors, under power-line conditions and +19 dBm
TX power, over 90% packet delivery success was maintained up to 650 m. Furthermore,
communication was sustained across a lake over 2.3 km and from an observation tower up to
2.47 km, demonstrating the benefits of elevated TX and favorable terrain.
   For indoor scenarios, the 3GPP TR 38.901 InH-LOS model best matched the measurements,
while outdoor results showed lower path loss than Okumura-Hata and COST-231 models.
Forests (NLOS) and high-traffic areas increased path loss, whereas environments with metal-
coated structures introduced a tunneling effect that reduced it. RSSI and SNR values associated
with over 90% packet success were observed above -90 dBm and around 11-12 dB indoors,
and -95 dBm and 12-15 dB outdoors. The study concludes that DECT-2020 NR supports low-
cost, battery-efficient deployments suitable for smart cities and industrial automation, achieving
several hundred meters of reliable range at low TX power and modest antenna height.

2.2 DECT-2020 NR for professional live audio: design space exploration and practical
                                      evaluation

Published in 2024, this study [2] explores the feasibility of using DECT-2020 NR for professional
live audio streaming, focusing on its low-latency capabilities. It discusses PHY-layer design
trade-offs between latency, Radio Frequency (RF) resource usage, and reliability. Experiments
were conducted using Nordic’s nRF9151 development kits and DECT-2020 NR modems.
   The PHY layer was implemented in the 1.9 GHz DECT band using a 1.728 MHz bandwidth.
Precise time-stamping with nanosecond resolution, synchronized via Pulse-Per-Second (PPS)
signals, enabled accurate latency measurements. The inter-Serial Peripheral Interface (SPI)
latency, defined as the time between TX initiation and successful packet RX, was recorded
across 20000 packets for each configuration, with antennas placed 30 cm apart in an open
environment. Results showed a minimum measured audio latency of 2.312 ms when using a 2
sub-slot TX duration and a 1.25 ms periodicity at MCS 1. This configuration consumed 33% of
RF resources. A more robust configuration yielded a latency of 2.517 ms using a 3 sub-slot TX
duration at MCS 0, occupying 50% of the RF channel.
   Throughput measurements under various MCS levels and TX durations showed that MCS
4 with 2 sub-slot TX and 0.21 ms periodicity achieved a throughput of 2246.4 kbit/s. The
minimum PHY-layer latency was measured as 1.062 ms using 6 sub-slot periodicity at MCS 1.
Attempts to lower latency below 1 ms encountered modem scheduling limitations, where the
modem failed to process frequent TX requests.
   The study also emphasizes that increasing TX duration or decreasing periodicity raises
channel occupancy. Only certain PHY configurations met the targeted 200 kbit/s data rate with
tolerable latency. Note that MAC features were not used, and audio codec or interference effects
were not included. The paper concludes that DECT-2020 NR can theoretically support sub-3 ms
wireless audio streaming. However, the current Nordic modem only supports a limited subset
of PHY features and does not yet fulfill the strict sub-ms latency demands of professional audio.


         2.3 Assessment of candidate technology ETSI: DECT-2020 New Radio

This independent evaluation [3] published in 2020, assesses DECT-2020 NR as submitted to the
Radiocommunication Sector of the International Telecommunication Union (ITU-R) by ETSI.
The focus is on latency, reliability, and connection density for mMTC.
   Based on ITU-R reports M.2410 and M.2412, and modeling from DECT Forum’s self-
evaluation reports, the paper states that user-plane one-way latency targets of < 1 ms for URLLC
are only achievable when Hybrid Automatic Repeat reQuest (HARQ) is disabled. Reported
latencies include:
    • 0.5616 ms (half slot, no HARQ)
    • 0.9776 ms (full slot, no HARQ)
    • 1.2272 ms (full slot, with HARQ)
   It concludes that DECT-2020 NR meets latency goals only in limited configurations and does
not fully meet all IMT-2020 requirements as of current evaluations.

   2.4 System reference document (SRdoc); DECT-2020 NR technology operating in
                             frequency bands below 6 GHz

The ETSI TR 103 722 [4], published by ETSI in 2024, provides a comprehensive overview of
DECT-2020 NR. Section 7.1.1 of the document confirms that the technology reuses the trans-
mission structure of classic DECT to maximize spectrum efficiency and minimize interference
in the 1880-1900 MHz band.
    Under open-space conditions with +19 dBm TX power and 1.728 MHz bandwidth, commu-
nication ranges of up to 6 km are reported. DECT-2020 NR supports automatic interference
management via channel selection, Listen Before Talk (LBT), and power control, enabling flex-
ible local deployments without centralized frequency planning. Table 7.1.2-1 in the document
presents PHY latency metrics:
    • With 27 kHz subcarrier spacing (𝜇), 1.728 MHz bandwidth, 416.667 (𝜇𝑠) TX duration,
       and MCS 1 (no HARQ), a one-way user plane latency of 0.90 ms is achievable.
    • With HARQ (10% retransmission), latency increases to 1.05 ms.
    • Using a higher scaling factor (𝜇 = 108 kHz), 6.912 MHz bandwidth, and MCS 2, one-way
       latency drops to 0.22 ms with a 104.2 𝜇𝑠 TX duration.


      2.5 Examination of PHY layer features in DECT-2020 and LTE-M networks

This study [5] compares DECT-2020 NR and LTE-M, both targeted at IoT applications. It
highlights that DECT-2020 NR can achieve up to 12.47 Mbit/s using 1024 Quadrature Amplitude
Modulation (QAM) with 1.728 MHz bandwidth and 27 kHz subcarrier spacing, surpassing
LTE-M’s maximum of 5 Mbit/s with 64-QAM. In terms of range, DECT-2020 NR is capable
of achieving approximately 2.4 km range using coded Binary Phase Shift Keying (BPSK), and
further extendable via mesh networking. The paper also notes that sub-10 ms latencies are
achievable in multi-hop mesh topologies.
   The study concludes that DECT-2020 NR is optimized for low-latency, high node-density,
and local-area mesh networking, making it suitable for industrial IoT and smart city applica-
tions. While it offers higher PHY data rates and spectral efficiency than LTE-M, its direct
communications range is shorter without mesh extension.

                          3    TECHNICAL BACKGROUND

This chapter provides the technical foundation for the experimental work presented in this thesis.
It begins by introducing the DECT-2020 NR standard, developed by ETSI, which serves as a
modern, non-cellular radio interface targeting URLLC and mMTC in the IoT domain. Following
this, the chapter focuses on DECT NR+, a proprietary implementation of the DECT-2020 NR
standard by Nordic Semiconductor, tailored for their nRF91 series SiP devices. Key architectural
components, protocol layers, PHY-layer structure, and configuration aspects specific to DECT
NR+ are discussed in detail. These elements are essential for understanding the communication
performance evaluations conducted in later chapters.


                    3.1 Network topologies and associated mechanisms

DECT-2020 NR supports several topologies:
  • Point-to-point and point-to-multipoint: A Fixed Termination (FT) device coordinates the
    radio resources, while Portable Terminations (PTs) associate with it.
  • Single-cell and multi-cell: One FT serves a cell, while in multi-cell setups, multiple FTs
    serve different areas and support roaming.
  • Mesh networking: Each Radio Device (RD) can act as a FT, PT, or both. Devices can
    autonomously route, forward, or receive data without a central coordinator.
  • Clustered tree topology: Formed when a backend-connected FT begins starts beaconing.
    Other RDs associate based on signal quality and routing cost.




            Figure 3.1. Formation of the clustered tree mesh network topology [6].

   The clustered tree topology in DECT-2020 NR is formed through a distributed association
process, illustrated in Figure 3.1. An RD with internet access, operating in FT mode initiates the
topology by beaconing its availability and backend connectivity. This beacon includes timing,
channel usage, and routing parameters. Nearby RDs evaluate the beacon and autonomously
choose to associate based on the signal quality and routing cost. Once associated, these RDs may
further act as intermediate nodes (RD𝐹𝑇,𝑃𝑇 ), enabling multi-hop expansion of the topology. The
process continues recursively, with new RDs joining based on local evaluations. The network can
include multiple FT mode RDs connected to different backends, allowing flexible and scalable
                                                                                              16


mesh formation across clustered regions. This decentralized, flexible design supports dynamic
and scalable deployments with autonomous routing and local link optimization.


                             3.2 DECT-2020 NR protocol stack

DECT-2020 NR uses a modular protocol stack [7],[8]:
  • PHY-layer: Handles transmission, modulation, coding, synchronization.
  • MAC-layer: Manages access, LBT, scheduling, HARQ, and power control.
  • Data Link Control (DLC) layer: Enables reliable peer communication and routing.
  • Convergence (CVG) layer: Maps application protocols (e.g., IPv6) to lower layers.
  • Application layer: Supports user-defined applications.


                                  3.3 PHY layer overview

The PHY layer of DECT-2020 NR handles essential signal processing operations that enable
reliable wireless communication [9]. It supports subcarrier spacings ranging from 27 kHz
to 216 kHz and uses scalable Fast Fourier Transform (FFT) sizes, which allow configurable
RF bandwidths from 1.728 MHz up to 221.184 MHz. A wide range of modulation schemes
is supported, including BPSK, Quadrature Phase Shift Keying (QPSK), 16-QAM, 64-QAM,
256-QAM, and 1024-QAM, providing flexibility to adapt to different data rate and robustness
requirements.
   In terms of functionality, the PHY layer performs modulation and demodulation to convert
data between digital and RF domains. It employs turbo coding with a rate of 1/3 using two 8-state
encoders to introduce redundancy and enhance error resilience. Forward Error Correction (FEC)
and Cyclic Redundancy Check (CRC) techniques are applied to detect and correct bit errors
during TX. Time and frequency synchronization processes ensure precise alignment between
communicating devices. Additionally, support for advanced techniques such as Multiple-Input
Multiple-Output (MIMO), beamforming, and diversity improves link quality and throughput,
especially in complex radio environments.
   The PHY layer also defines two main channels: the Physical Control Channel (PCC), which
carries signaling and control information, and the Physical Data Channel (PDC), which transmits
user payloads. These channels work together to enable coordinated and efficient data exchange
across the network.

                            3.3.5 Coexistence and spectrum use

To ensure coexistence between DECT-2020 NR systems and legacy DECT technologies, several
design principles have been maintained. The smallest nominal channel bandwidth in DECT-
2020 NR remains the same as that of classic DECT systems, i.e., 1.728 MHz. Wider nominal
bandwidths are implemented as integer multiples of 1.728 MHz, ensuring spectral alignment.
Additionally, the frame, slot, and sub-slot durations in DECT-2020 NR are harmonized with
those in legacy DECT systems. Transmission durations are defined as integer multiples of
sub-slot and slot lengths, further facilitating backward compatibility and spectrum coexistence.
DECT-2020 NR also includes advanced spectrum management features that enhance coexistence
capabilities, which include:
   • TX power control, which minimizes interference by adjusting power levels based on
      channel conditions.
   • RSSI-1 measurements, which enable the detection of any signals from other DECT systems
      operating on the same frequency band.
   • RSSI-2 measurements, used to detect and assess transmissions from other DECT-2020
      NR systems.
   These mechanisms collectively support efficient operation in shared spectrum environments
while minimizing interference with other DECT technologies.

                     3.3.6 Channel bands and frequency arrangement

The radio channel numbering scheme allows channel assignments across the frequency range of
450 MHz to 5875 MHz, organized into 17 distinct operating bands. An RD may support one or
more frequency bands depending on its hardware capabilities. In this study, band 1 was used,
which operates in the frequency range of 1880 MHz to 1900 MHz for both TX and RX.
According to the DECT-2020 NR channel raster, the center frequency (𝐹𝑐 ) for bands 1-12 and
22 is calculated using the formula[11]:


                                 𝐹𝑐 = 𝐹0 + (𝑛 × 0.864 MHz)                                (3.1)

Where,
   • 𝐹0 = 450.144 MHz
   • n = 1, 2, 3,..., 2951
   In this study, channels 1665 and 1667 were used, both of which fall within band 1. Their
corresponding center frequencies are:
   • Channel 1665: 𝐹𝑐 = 1888.704 MHz
   • Channel 1667: 𝐹𝑐 = 1890.432 MHz

channels. This overlap supports denser channel reuse and is particularly advantageous in mesh
and multi-link network scenarios.


                                      3.4 MAC layer

The MAC layer in DECT-2020 NR is responsible for managing a range of control functionali-
ties essential for establishing and maintaining radio communication. These include local radio
coordination, paging, broadcasting, random access, beacon scanning, and connection configu-
ration [7]. Additionally, the MAC layer handles channel selection, logical-to-transport channel
mapping, HARQ-based error correction, and MAC-layer security through integrity protection
and encryption. It also enables LBT and automatic TX power tuning to optimize communication
in shared spectrum environments.
   LBT is a key medium access mechanism used to reduce collisions. Before TX, an RD
performs a sensing operation to determine if the channel is idle. If the channel is busy, the
TX is deferred and a back-off procedure is triggered based on the MAC configuration. In
Nordic’s DECT NR+ implementation, as defined in the nrf_modem_dect_phy.h header, the
LBT duration is specified as a multiple of the OFDM symbol period (41.6 𝜇𝑠), ranging from
a minimum of 2 symbols (83.3 𝜇𝑠) to a maximum of 110 symbols (4583 𝜇𝑠). While LBT
improves coexistence, it introduces a variable delay before TX, which can affect latency and
throughput, particularly under heavy traffic. In this study, LBT was disabled during latency-
sensitive measurements to ensure more deterministic timing.
   HARQ with incremental redundancy is optionally supported in DECT-2020 NR thus DECT
NR+ to improve reliability through retransmissions at the MAC layer. However, HARQ was not
enabled in this work. Instead only single attempt TX communication was used to isolate and
evaluate pure PHY layer performance. Key identifiers used in the MAC layer include:
    • 32 bit Network Identifier (ID) - The upper 24 bits ensure global uniqueness, while the
       lower 8 bits help avoid local collisions.
                                                                                            20


   • 32 bit Long Radio Device ID (Long RD ID) - Used for network-wide unique device
     identification during association, routing, and MAC-level security.
   • 16 bit Short Radio Device ID (Short RD ID) - Assigned locally for PHY-layer identification
     and exchanged during association to link with the Long RD ID.


                                  3.5 Transmission power

Transmit power significantly affects the communication range. Higher transmission power
improves the RX signal strength, thereby increasing the link budget and extending the effective
range specially under LOS conditions. The nRF9161 modem supports a set of discrete transmit
power levels ranging from -12 dBm to +19 dBm. While DECT-2020 NR specification does
not explicitly define an automatic power control mechanism, the DECT NR+ implementation
includes the capability to perform automatic power adjustment at the MAC layer, typically
based on RSSI or SNR feedback from receiver side. However, this feature was not used in the
experiments conducted in this study. Instead, transmit power levels were manually configured
at the PHY layer to isolate the effect of TX power on range.


                       3.6 PHY control via Nordic modem library

The experiments in this thesis were implemented using Nordic Semiconductor’s nrf_modem
library [12] on the nRF9161 SiP. Key Application Programming Interfaces (APIs) used include:
    • nrf_modem_dect_phy_init() - initialize DECT PHY interface.
    • nrf_modem_dect_phy_deinit() - de-initialize DECT PHY interface.
    • nrf_modem_dect_phy_tx() - schedule a TX.
    • nrf_modem_dect_phy_rx() - schedule a RX.
    • nrf_modem_dect_phy_callback_set() - set application callbacks for PHY opera-
       tions.
    • nrf_modem_dect_phy_time_get() - retrieve 64-bit modem time (15 ns resolution).
    • nrf_modem_dect_phy_rx_stop() - stop an ongoing RX.
    • nrf_modem_dect_phy_rssi() - schedule an RSSI measurement operation.
   TX and RX operations were controlled using tx_op_params and rx_op_params, respec-
tively. These structures allowed configuration of network ID, PHY mode, power level, LBT
status, and reception duration. The op_complete() callback is used to indicate the successful
completion of a scheduled modem operation.

The DECT Shell (DeSh) is a command-line utility included in Nordic Semiconductor’s DECT
NR+ sample applications within the nRF connect SDK. It is built on the Zephyr RTOS and
provides an interactive environment for configuring, controlling, and evaluating the DECT NR+
PHY layer on the nRF91 SiPs, particularly the nRF9161. The shell interfaces directly with the
nrf_modem_dect_phy API and allows real-time testing of radio performance through various
commands. For the purpose of this thesis, only a selected set of commands was used. These

PHY-layer throughput, and RTT latency, respectively.

                                      3.7.1 Perf command

The perf command is used to evaluate the PHY layer throughput and link status between two
devices. One board operates as the server, receiving data, while the other functions as the
client, transmitting data. At the end of the test session, performance metrics such as RSSI,
SNR, effective throughput, and packet error count are reported. The server is initiated by first
assigning a TX ID using,
     dect sett -t 39
The server is then set to RX using,
     dect perf -s -t 15 --channel 1665
Here, the -s option sets the server mode, -t defines the listening duration (with -1 used for
continuous RX), and –channel specifies the operating frequency. On the client side, a test
session is initiated using,
     dect perf -c --s_tx_id 39 --c_tx_mcs 4 --c_slots 4 -t 15
     --channel 1665
Where,
   • -c: enables client mode.
   • –s_tx_id: TX ID of the server.
   • –c_tx_mcs: TX MCS level.
   • –c_tx_pwr: TX power level.
   • –c_slots: packet length in TX slots.
   • -t: duration of the test in seconds.
   • –channel: selected DECT NR+ channel.
   This command was primarily used in maximum range experiments and for collecting key
performance metrics of the DECT NR+ link.


                                3.7.2 RSSI scanning command

The rssi_scan commands performs a scan across selected channels and reports the received
signal strength. It can be configured with several options, such as:
   • -c: specifies the channel to scan.
   • -t: sets the scan duration in milliseconds.
   • –verdict_type_count: enables sub-slot based classification of channel usage.
An example use of this command is,
     dect rssi_scan -c 0 --verdict_type_count -t 3000
These scans were performed at test locations to identify usable frequency bands and avoid
congested channels.

The ping command measures the RTT between two nodes. One board is configured as the
server, which listens and responds to ping requests, while the other acts as the client that initiates
the requests. The server is started with,

and the client is started with,

                    3.8 Comparison with Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE)

Among existing radio access technologies, Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) is one of the closest
in scope and functionality to DECT NR+, making it a relevant benchmark for performance
comparison. While BLE is a mature and widely adopted protocol, DECT NR+ is a newer
technology targeting similar application domains.
   BLE and DECT NR+ share several characteristics:
   • Operation in license-exempt bands.
   • Targeting low power and short-range communication.
   • Support for mesh networking architectures.
   • Comparable use cases of smart sensors, industrial automation and audio streaming.
   Although they differ in their PHY layer implementations, BLE operates in the 2.4 GHz band,
while DECT NR+ uses OFDM based communication in the 1.9 GHz and other sub-6 GHz
bands. Given their shared design objectives, BLE serves as a suitable point of reference. For
this reason, DECT NR+ performance is compared with BLE in terms of communication range,
data throughput, latency, and energy efficiency in Chapter 5.

                           4.2.3 Minimum latency measurement

This experiment focused on determining the lowest achievable RTT using a custom echo-based
communication mechanism between two DECT NR+ nodes as illustrated in Figure 4.6. The
minimum latency tests were also conducted in the same indoor environment as described in
Chapter 4.2.2.




                          Figure 4.6. Minimum latency experiment.

   To evaluate communication latency, Nordic’s DeSh firmware was initially used with the
ping command. However, the RTT values obtained using this method were relatively high
(approximately 80 ms). To optimize for minimal latency, a custom firmware was developed. In
the custom RTT measurement application, as illustrated in Figure 4.7, the client node begins
by retrieving the current modem time ticks (t1) and embedding it in the transmit buffer along
with a packet ID. This packet is then sent to the server node. Immediately after TX, the client
switches back to RX mode to wait for the echoed response. The server node operates as a
passive echo node. Upon receiving a packet, it immediately stops the ongoing reception using
the nrf_modem_dect_phy_rx_stop() command and transmits the same packet back without
any further processing. Once the client receives the echoed packet, it extracts the original
timestamp (t1) and compares it with the timestamp at RX (t2), which is captured using the
pdc() callback. The RTT is then calculated as the time taken for the packet to travel from client
to server and back.
   To ensure reliable modem scheduling and avoid operation conflicts, a short intentional delay
was introduced at server node’s switching between RX to TX mode. Empirical testing revealed

This chapter presents and discusses the experimental results obtained through the testbed im-

communication under various real-world conditions by addressing the four main objectives
outlined in this study:
    • Maximum communication range of a single hop
    • Maximum achievable throughput
    • Minimum RTT

                     5.2.1 Throughput performance using Nordic’s DeSh

As discussed earlier in Chapter 4.2.2, DeSh firmware includes a built-in scheduling delay between
consecutive transmissions, controlled via the –c_gap_subslots parameter. This parameter in-

waveform analysis using the PPK2 revealed that this configuration introduces a transmission gap
of approximately 830 𝜇s between consecutive packets. By adjusting the –c_gap_subslots
parameter to 3, the TX gap was reduced to approximately 630 𝜇s, resulting in an improved
throughput of approximately 2450 kbit/s, as illustrated in Figure 5.10. Attempts to reduce this
delay further (e.g., setting the parameter to 1 or 2) resulted in modem scheduling errors, likely
due to modem handling delays.

they do so at different rates. Since throughput is defined as the ratio of TBS to total TX time,
this mismatch in growth rates results in the nonlinearity of the throughput curve. Specifically,
in lower sub-slot configurations, the TBS increases more rapidly than the transmission time,
resulting in a sharp rise in throughput. However, as the number of sub-slots increases, the growth
in TBS becomes more gradual relative to the growth in time, leading to a plateauing effect. This
behavior gives rise to the concave shape of the throughput curve, despite both parameters
appearing to increase linearly. This behavior can be better understood by referring to the MCS
configurations summarized in Table 3.1. In DECT NR+, transitions from MCS 1 to 2 and from
MCS 3 to 4 involve changes in coding rate only, without any increase in modulation order. As
a result, the TBS growth between those MCS levels is limited. Understanding this nonlinear
relationship is particularly important when optimizing PHY configurations, as it highlights that
allocating additional sub-slots does not always yield proportional throughput gains, especially
at higher sub-slot counts.
   According to the literature, BLE 5.0 achieves a maximum theoretical throughput of approx-
imately 1.4 Mbit/s [14]. In comparison, this study demonstrates that DECT NR+ significantly
outperforms BLE in terms of maximum achievable throughput, reaching up to 2.5 Mbit/s under
optimal conditions with the current nRF9161 implementation. While these results already sur-
pass some values reported in prior studies, such as the 2246.4 kbit/s [2] throughput achieved in
one implementation, as described in Chapter 2.2, they remain below certain analytical estimates
found in literature. For example, a theoretical upper-bound throughput of 12.47 Mbit/s [5] was
predicted under ideal conditions, assuming the use of higher-order modulation schemes like
1024-QAM, as discussed in Chapter 2.5. In future DECT NR+ implementations, support for
additional MCS levels and higher-order modulations such as 1024-QAM as outlined in the ETSI
specification [6] could enable significantly higher data rates. Nevertheless, the measured perfor-
mance is already satisfactory given DECT NR+ is not primarily intended for high-throughput
applications. Instead, the technology is optimized for low-latency, high-reliability communi-
cation in dense device environments. However, unlocking higher modulation levels in future
hardware or firmware updates could allow DECT NR+ to evolve into a more versatile and
                                                                                               45


well-balanced communication solution.

                    6    CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK

This chapter summarizes the key findings of the research, evaluates how the objectives were met,
and outlines potential directions for future work. The primary focus of this thesis was the real-
world performance evaluation of DECT NR+ at the PHY layer using Nordic Semiconductor’s
nRF9161 development kits.
  The experimental work focused on four main objectives:
   • Maximum communication range of a single hop
   • Maximum achievable throughput
   • Minimum latency (based on RTT)
   • Energy efficiency estimation


                                6.1 Summary of key findings

For the maximum communication range evaluation, Nordic’s sample application DeSh was
used. The key performance indicator was the PDR, the ratio of successfully received packets to
transmitted packets. Tests were conducted in both indoor and outdoor environments. In addition
to PDR, variations in RSSI and SNR were also observed.
    • In outdoor LOS conditions, the maximum range achieved was 730 meters, maintaining
       approximately 80% PDR at a TX power of +19 dBm using MCS 0.
    • Under less favorable outdoor conditions, environmental factors such as interference and
       partial obstructions reduced the reliable range to approximately 400 meters, despite using
       the same configuration.
    • Indoors, the maximum range achieved was 49 meters with 80% PDR using +4 dBm TX
       power and MCS 4.
   For the maximum achievable throughput, the firmware was optimized to eliminate software-
included delays and reveal the upper limits constrained by the modem itself. By analyzing
high-current bursts during TX, it was determined that the minimum scheduling gap between
transmissions was approximately 570 𝜇𝑠. Under these conditions, using MCS 4 and transmitting
for 8 sub-slots, a peak throughput of 2498.70 kbit/s was achieved. This value reflects the data
channel throughput only, excluding PHY layer overheads. A comprehensive set of throughput
measurements for all supported MCS/sub-slot combinations was also presented. In the latency
analysis, RTT measurements were performed using a custom implementation based on modem
time-stamping. The minimum RTT recorded was 2.255 ms, implying a one-way PHY-layer
latency of 1.127 ms under MCS 1, +19 dBm TX power, LBT disabled, and a TX duration of 2
sub-slots. Additionally, an ideal one-way latency estimation using modem-reported delays was
found to be approximately 950 𝜇𝑠.
For energy efficiency, energy per bit was calculated across various MCS and sub-slot configu-
rations. Further analysis was conducted on TX mode power behavior and system-level PAPR
using Nordic’s PPK2. The results show that the minimum energy consumption achieved was
0.26 𝜇𝐽/𝑏𝑖𝑡 at MCS 4, while the average RX power consumption was approximately 200 mW.
   In summary, the experimental results demonstrated that DECT NR+ is capable of achieving:
    • A maximum range of 730 meters with 80% PDR at a throughput of 368.05 kbit/s and 655
       meters with 100% PDR.
    • A maximum throughput of 2498.70 kbit/s.
    • A minimum RTT of 2.255 ms.
    • A minimum energy consumption of 0.26 𝜇𝐽/𝑏𝑖𝑡.
    • A minimum average TX power of 390 mW and average RX power of 200 mW.
                                                                                                   53


    These results highlight that DECT NR+ is a strong potential candidate for URLLC oriented
applications, particularly in time-sensitive wireless communication environments. Compared to
BLE, DECT NR+ demonstrated superior performance in communication range, peak throughput,
and latency. While BLE typically achieves LOS ranges of 200-400 meters and a maximum
theoretical throughput of 1.4 Mbit/s, DECT NR+ maintained communication up to 730 meters
with 80% PDR and achieved up to 2.5 Mbit/s throughput. However, BLE remains more energy-
efficient, consuming as little as 0.075 𝜇𝐽/𝑏𝑖𝑡 compared to the best-case 0.26 𝜇𝐽/𝑏𝑖𝑡 in this
study. This highlights a trade-off between energy consumption and range/throughput, where
DECT NR+ is more suitable for long-range, high-throughput, and low-latency use cases.
    The experimental results are also in line with several prior studies and theoretical expectations.
For example, previous work reported RTT values of around 1.95-2.12 ms, comparable to the
2.264 ms mean RTT measured in this study. Similarly, reported maximum throughput values
near 2.2 Mbit/s and observed RSSI, SNR thresholds for 100% PDR match the trends captured
here. However, other studies have demonstrated multi-kilometer range using optimized antennas
and elevated deployments. The modest range observed in this study, limited to 730 meters, is
attributed to hardware constraints and environmental conditions, such as low antenna height and
urban interference. Despite this, the findings validate DECT NR+ as a reliable URLLC-capable
PHY solution in practical embedded applications.


                                         6.2 Limitations

This research was limited to point-to-point PHY-layer evaluations using Nordic’s currently
available modem firmware. Features such as MAC-layer functionality, multi-node communica-
tions, interference handling, mesh networking, and HARQ-based retransmission strategies were
not explored. All measurements were performed using the built-in antennas on the nRF9161
development kits.


                                        6.3 Future work

Future studies can expand upon this work by:
   • Evaluating MAC-layer performance and scheduling algorithms.
   • Evaluating performance under high-density deployments and interference-prone environ-
      ments.
   • Implementing and testing multi-hop mesh topologies to study scalability and latency
      trends.
   • Exploring how HARQ mechanisms influence range, throughput, and latency.
   • Assessing the impact of using external antennas, supported via the development kit’s
      external antenna port, on communication performance.
   This thesis contributes one of the first experimental evaluations of DECT NR+, establishing
a foundational understanding of its PHY-layer capabilities. As wireless systems continue to
evolve, DECT NR+ shows promise as a flexible and efficient solution for future URLLC and
mMTC applications.
