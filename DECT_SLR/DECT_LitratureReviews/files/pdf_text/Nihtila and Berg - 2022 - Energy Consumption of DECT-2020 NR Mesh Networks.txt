     A.         Network organization
        The DECT-2020 NR standard explains how the network
     organizes itself. For a single network, there is always one
     sink, i.e. a gateway node to the Internet. There can be
     multiple sinks in a group of nodes, but from the system
     point of view, the sinks form their own networks (cluster
     trees), which can co-exist, but operate independently from                       Fig. 1 Simple example of a star topology with a sink and 6 leaf nodes.
     each other. In this study, we concentrate on the operation of
     a single network entity under one sink.
        Organization starts by the sink selecting FT (Fixed
     Termination point) mode. In FT mode, a node first performs
     background scanning, searching for the least busy channel,
     out of the available DECT channels, for operation. After
     finding a channel, the FT starts beaconing on it.
        All other nodes are in PT (Portable Termination point)
     mode, where they initially scan channels for beacons and
     after detecting one or more of them, select from them the
     sender FT for association. A node having multiple FT
     candidates makes the choice based on the route cost. Exact
     route cost calculation is left to implementation, but
     essentially the node is free to use any information available.
     In this study, the nodes select the strongest beaconing FT
     for association.
        After a PT has successfully associated with an FT, the PT
     switches to FT mode and performs operations as explained                         Fig. 2 Simple example of a 4-tier mesh topology consisting of a sink, 5
     for the FT mode above, in order to invite potential children                     routers and 8 leaf nodes.
     and by that, extend the coverage area of the network.

     B.         Network topology
         The finished network can be thought of as nested node
     tiers, each tier adding a node-to-node hop for the traffic
     traveling towards the sink.
         Depending on the network characteristics, such as node
     density, scenario size, beacon transmission power and
     required beacon quality for association, there can be only
     one or multiple tiers in the network, forming either a star or
     a mesh topology.
     1)        Star topology: A single-tier network where all
     nodes are within sink coverage and associate directly with
     it. All nodes apart from the sink are leaf nodes and do not
     have any other role. A simple example of a star topology is                      Fig. 3 Simple example of data forwarding from a leaf to the sink via a
