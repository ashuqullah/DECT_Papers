Title: DECT-2020 NR for Energy and Power Grid

Summary:

This paper addresses the central research challenge of the selected DECT-2020 NR / 5G / wireless networking domain. It focuses on how the proposed design or algorithm improves reliability, coverage, resource management, or performance for emerging URLLC, mMTC, or mesh-networked applications.

Problem and motivation:
—Modern power grids and industrial systems require scalable and reliable
wireless connectivity to support real-time monitoring and control. DECT-2020 New
Radio (NR), an ETSIstandardized wireless technology operating in license-exempt
spectrum, offers native mesh networking, low latency, and flexible deployment
for industrial Internet-of-Things (IoT) applications. This paper investigates
the suitability of DECT-2020 NR mesh networking for power grid monitoring within
the Ultra Scalable Wireless Access (USWA) project. Two proof-of-concept (PoC)
deployments are evaluated, collecting data from multiple sensors under varying
conditions, including packet fragmentation, multihop communication, and
different data rates to assess the endto-end performance of the system in terms
of quality-of-service (QoS) metrics. From the results, we found that DECT-2020
NR shows potential for larger scale energy and substation monitoring
deployments.

Digitization of industries requires efficient, flexible, and cost-effective
wireless connectivity solutions. Many industrial facilities still rely on cables
for communication, which limits scalability and reconfiguration. DECT-2020 New
Radio (NR) – a modern wireless ETSI standard – has emerged as a promising
candidate to address this gap. DECT-2020 NR offers a stateof-the-art radio
interface tailored for industrial Internet-ofThings (IoT) applications,
including native mesh networking capabilities that enable large-scale local
networks through multihop relaying between devices, as well as point-to-point
and star network topologies. Operating in a license-exempt band (the frequency
band previously used by Digital Enhanced Cordless Telecommunication devices,
i.e., legacy DECT), it allows industrial users to deploy and manage private
networks without licensing costs, opening opportunities for more autonomous and
scalable digitization efforts in factories, buildings, utility sites, and
cities. This paper is conducted within the framework of the Ultra Scalable
Wireless Access (USWA) project (project running from 12/2022 to 11/2025), which
investigated how advanced DECT2020 NR features can support diverse industrial
use cases. The project goals included defining system architectures and
performance requirements, developing scalable and energy-efficient mesh
networking for massive machine type communications (mMTC) IoT deployments,

Contributions and implications:
The work contributes practical insight for DECT-2020 NR and related next-generation wireless systems. It provides experimental or simulated evidence for the proposed scheme and highlights how the results support stricter reliability, lower latency, or improved resource utilization in private network or industrial IoT scenarios.
