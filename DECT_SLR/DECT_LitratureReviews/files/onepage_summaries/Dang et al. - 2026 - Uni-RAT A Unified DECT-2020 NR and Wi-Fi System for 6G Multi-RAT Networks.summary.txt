Title: Uni-RAT: A Unified DECT-2020 NR and Wi-Fi

Summary:

This paper addresses the central research challenge of the selected DECT-2020 NR / 5G / wireless networking domain. It focuses on how the proposed design or algorithm improves reliability, coverage, resource management, or performance for emerging URLLC, mMTC, or mesh-networked applications.

Problem and motivation:
—Future 6G networks aim to deliver seamless, secure, and low-latency
connectivity across heterogeneous radio access technologies (RATs) such as 5G
New Radio (NR), Wi-Fi, and DECT-2020 NR to support emerging applications
including extended reality (XR) and the Industrial Internet of Things (IIoT).
However, current Multi-RATs systems are operated and managed in isolation, which
hinders users and devices from seamlessly leveraging heterogeneous connectivity
in a unified and efficient manner. To address this fragmentation, 3GPP has
introduced interworking functions such as the N3IWF for untrusted and the TNGF
for trusted non-3GPP access, enabling IP-session continuity when users move
across heterogeneous networks. Nevertheless, these mechanisms rely on mandatory
IPsec, which requires each RAT to maintain a separate secure tunnel to the core
network and prevents intra-cell device-todevice (D2D) communication, thereby
introducing significant encryption latency, increasing data-plane load, and
complicating network management. To overcome these limitations, we propose Uni-
RAT, a unified Multi-RATs framework in which DECT2020 NR operates as the Master-
RAT, anchoring 3GPP controlplane security via 5G-AKA and NAS, while Wi-Fi
functions as a Secondary-RAT for data-plane data delivery. Uni-RAT employs
lightweight Layer-3 WireGuard tunnels to secure both D2D and multi-hop
communication, significantly reducing the latency and computational overhead of
IPsec-based interworking mechanisms used by N3IWF and TNGF. In addition, a
customized SMS-over-NAS signaling mechanism is introduced to distribute
WireGuard configuration and keying material, enabling unified authentication and
efficient Multi-RATs security management. We implement a full prototype using
Nordic nRF9161 DECT-2020 NR devices, Raspberry Pi Wi-Fi nodes, and a Cumucore 5G
core, with security-critical functions protected inside Arm TrustZone.
Experimental results indicate that Uni-RAT supports Multi-RATs communication
with high throughput and low packet loss in intra-cell D2D scenarios, maintains
bounded latency and jitter under relay-assisted and inter-cell configurations,
and achieves stable UE registration latency below 100 ms with up to 100
concurrent devices under background traffic, demonstrating its practical
feasibility and scalability and positioning Uni-RAT as an early architectural
blueprint for 6G Multi-RATs networks.

In the untrusted non-3GPP access model, the non-3GPP interworking function
(N3IWF) connects the user equipment (UE) to the 5GC via an end-to-end (E2E)
IPsec tunnel that secures both NAS signaling and data-plane traffic. In
contrast, the trusted non3GPP access model employs the Trusted non-3GPP gateway
function (TNGF), which enables direct NAS signaling between the UE and the
access and mobility management function (AMF) while relying on trusted link-
layer security mechanisms, such as IEEE 802.1X or MACsec. While both N3IWF and
TNGF enable non-3GPP access to the 5GC, they are fundamentally designed to
integrate each non-3GPP RAT independently through per-access secure connectivity
to the core network. Consequently, security contexts are not shared across RATs,
which prevents coordinated cross-RAT control and results in fragmented control-
plane operation. Moreover, although these mechanisms ensure E2E integrity and
confidentiality, their reliance on IPsec tunneling or link-layer security

Contributions and implications:
The work contributes practical insight for DECT-2020 NR and related next-generation wireless systems. It provides experimental or simulated evidence for the proposed scheme and highlights how the results support stricter reliability, lower latency, or improved resource utilization in private network or industrial IoT scenarios.
