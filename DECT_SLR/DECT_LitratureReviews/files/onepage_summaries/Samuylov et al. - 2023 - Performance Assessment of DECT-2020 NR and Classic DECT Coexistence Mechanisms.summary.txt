Summary:

This paper addresses the central research challenge of the selected DECT-2020 NR / 5G / wireless networking domain. It focuses on how the proposed design or algorithm improves reliability, coverage, resource management, or performance for emerging URLLC, mMTC, or mesh-networked applications.

Problem and motivation:
—The recently standardized ETSI DECT-2020 New Radio (NR) technology promises to
enable operator-independent Internet-of-Things (IoT) services. One of the
supported operating bands is the 1880 − 1900 MHz band where IoT devices use one
or more 1.728 MHz wide channels for multi-hop communications. It has been shown
that such systems scale well to satisfy the requirements of 5G massive machine-
type communications (mMTC). During the standardization process, the coexistence
with classic DECT technology was addressed but the benefits of different options
have not been deeply explored. In this paper, by utilizing system-level
simulation techniques we evaluate several coexistence solutions for DECT-2020
and classic DECT systems including conventional listen-before-talk access (LBT),
last-minute scan, and scheduling-based mechanisms. Our numerical results
illustrate that the standard LBT operation ensures excellent performance with no
more than 2% of packet drops for classic DECT, and still results in satisfactory
DECT2020 operation under 25% of resources allocated to classic DECT. The use of
last-minute-scan leads to drastic performance degradation of DECT-2020 (by more
than 10% in terms of packet drops) as compared to the standard LBT-only
operation. Finally, a scheduling-based mechanism allows to improve the LBT
performance for DECT-2020 devices by 2-5%.

NTRODUCTION The amount of services offered by the Internet of Things (IoT) is
growing each year spanning all the commercial sectors [1]. To enable
connectivity for constantly growing IoT equipment massive machine-type
communications (mMTC) technologies are being specified as a part of 5G IMT-2020.
NB-IoT and LTE-M are the main mMTC technologies developed by 3GPP to provide
cellular IoT (CIoT) connectivity with the former satisfying 5G IMT-2020
requirements [2]. In addition to 3GPP activities, there is an alternative ETSI
initiative standardizing non-CIoT 5G mMTC technology. These efforts recently
commenced in ETSI DECT-2020 New Radio (NR) standard in 2021 [3]. Current
specifications define 19 different operating bands for DECT-2020 NR below 6 GHz,
including unlicensed DECT bands [4]. The use of

Contributions and implications:
The work contributes practical insight for DECT-2020 NR and related next-generation wireless systems. It provides experimental or simulated evidence for the proposed scheme and highlights how the results support stricter reliability, lower latency, or improved resource utilization in private network or industrial IoT scenarios.
