Summary:

This paper addresses the central research challenge of the selected DECT-2020 NR / 5G / wireless networking domain. It focuses on how the proposed design or algorithm improves reliability, coverage, resource management, or performance for emerging URLLC, mMTC, or mesh-networked applications.

Problem and motivation:
—DECT-2020 New Radio (NR) is a radio interface technology (RIT) standardized by
the European Telecommunications Standards Institute (ETSI) for large-scale
massive machine-type communication (mMTC) and Ultra-Reliable and Low-Latency
Communications (URLLC) deployments. In DECT2020 NR mesh networks, multi-hop
delivery is typically realized by flooding-based forwarding, which is robust but
increases network load and does not explicitly optimize end-to-end latency or
reliability. In this paper, we propose a topology-aware Deep Reinforcement
Learning (DRL) framework for URLLC-oriented multi-hop routing in DECT-2020 NR
mesh networks. Nexthop selection is modeled as a Markov decision process (MDP)
whose state combines link-level latency and packet error rate (PER) metrics with
graph-structured topology information. An inductive graph neural network (GNN)
encoder summarizes the current mesh topology and a per-edge Deep Q-Network (DQN)
head selects the next hop using a URLLC-shaped reward that jointly captures end-
to-end latency and reliability. To improve scalability, the agent is trained
through a curriculum over progressively larger topologies. Simulation results
over small to large mesh topologies show that the proposed GNN+DRL routing
policy improves the URLLC trade-off compared with both the existing latency-only
routing method and the DECT2020 NR flooding procedure. For a representative
medium-sized mesh network, it reduces end-to-end latency by up to 19.9%, lowers
end-to-end PER by up to 10%, and decreases the number of transmissions per
delivered packet compared with the existing approach.
