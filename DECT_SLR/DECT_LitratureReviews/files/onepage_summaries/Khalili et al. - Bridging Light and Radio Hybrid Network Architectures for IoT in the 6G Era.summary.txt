Title: Bridging Light and Radio: Hybrid Network

Summary:

This paper addresses the central research challenge of the selected DECT-2020 NR / 5G / wireless networking domain. It focuses on how the proposed design or algorithm improves reliability, coverage, resource management, or performance for emerging URLLC, mMTC, or mesh-networked applications.

Problem and motivation:
The massive scale and dense deployments of future Internet of Things (IoT),
particularly within the 6G framework, expose the critical limitations of
traditional Radio Frequency (RF) systems, namely spectral congestion,
interference, and security vulnerabilities. Optical Wireless Communication (OWC)
oﬀers a compelling complement, providing vast, unlicensed spectrum, immunity to
RF interference, and enhanced physical-layer security. However, OWC's
susceptibility to blockages and limited mobility hinders its standalone
deployment, as it cannot, on its own, meet the stringent connectivity and
mobility requirements of many IoT applications. This article explores the
convergence of optical and radio networks and proposes a ﬂexible, adaptable, and
resilient connectivity framework tailored for modern IoT ecosystems. We present
a holistic system architecture that integrates single-mode RF, single-mode OWC,
and uniﬁed hybrid Access Points (APs) to eﬃciently manage the diverse and often
conﬂicting objectives of various IoT applications. While these hybrid RF/OWC APs
oﬀer signiﬁcant advantages in cost, eﬃciency, and coordinated management, they
also introduce a primary deployment challenge: the inherent coverage mismatch
between the two technologies. To precisely address this critical issue, we
demonstrate a practical solution supported by a coverage equivalence analysis to
validate the proposed framework. This analysis quantiﬁes the required OWC
transmitter density to match a given RF range, oﬀering tangible guidance for
system planning.

Contributions and implications:
The work contributes practical insight for DECT-2020 NR and related next-generation wireless systems. It provides experimental or simulated evidence for the proposed scheme and highlights how the results support stricter reliability, lower latency, or improved resource utilization in private network or industrial IoT scenarios.
