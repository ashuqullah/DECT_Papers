Title: European Wireless 2024 Ŏ 09 – 11 September 2024 Ŏ Brno, Czech Republic

Summary:

This paper addresses the central research challenge of the selected DECT-2020 NR / 5G / wireless networking domain. It focuses on how the proposed design or algorithm improves reliability, coverage, resource management, or performance for emerging URLLC, mMTC, or mesh-networked applications.

Problem and motivation:
—5G communication networks are suitable to realize Tactile Internet with Human
in the Loop application due to the envisaged latency promises. Currently, 5G
Release 15 is widely deployed. Latency performance to control robots or enable
humans to engage with the metaverse will require higher releases. WiFi is widely
used due to its cost, but has poor latency performance due to the “listen-
before-talk” requirement in the ISM bands. Therefore, solutions that combine low
cost and high latency performance are needed. This paper presents a detailed
performance evaluation of a 5G DECT NR+ testbed with a focus on latency and data
rate. While commercial off-the-shelf (COTS) 5G testbeds are still very
expensive, we present a low-cost solution to achieve similar or even better
latency performance with NR+. The testbed is composed of two NR+ platforms and
will explore different settings such as packet length and Modulation and Coding
Scheme (MCS). The main result of the paper is that non-optimized COTS DECT NR+
can provide latency values of 2 ms to 8 ms and a data rate of 2.3 Mbit/s to 2.5
Mbit/s.

modem/doc/dectphy.html. VI. C ONCLUSION While 5G and future 6G deployments
provide highly accurate and ultra low latency timing for command and control
data required for the tactile internet, including robotics and metaverse
applications, this performance comes at a high cost for deployment. Our
measurements indicate that latency values as well as packet delay variations,
i.e., jitter, are all approaching the thresholds required for tactile internet
applications, such as robotics or the metaverse. Future evaluations will target
overall performance measurements including sustainable data rates. Further
research will target potentials for further latency reductions and combining
DECT-2020 NR+ with other approaches to derive a complete system implementation.

Contributions and implications:
The work contributes practical insight for DECT-2020 NR and related next-generation wireless systems. It provides experimental or simulated evidence for the proposed scheme and highlights how the results support stricter reliability, lower latency, or improved resource utilization in private network or industrial IoT scenarios.
