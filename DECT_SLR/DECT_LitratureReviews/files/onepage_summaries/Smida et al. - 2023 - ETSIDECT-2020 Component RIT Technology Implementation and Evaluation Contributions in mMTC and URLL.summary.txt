Title: ETSI/DECT-2020 Component RIT Technology

Summary:

This paper addresses the central research challenge of the selected DECT-2020 NR / 5G / wireless networking domain. It focuses on how the proposed design or algorithm improves reliability, coverage, resource management, or performance for emerging URLLC, mMTC, or mesh-networked applications.

Problem and motivation:
—We propose a comprehensive evaluation of ETSI/DECT-2020 New Radio (NR) as an
IMT-2020 candidate technology for the mMTC (connection density) and URLLC
(reliability) use cases. Several independent evaluation groups assessed
DECT-2020 NR performance against the IMT-2020 requirements and INRS played a key
role within the Canadian Evaluation Group (CEG) in evaluating the technology via
simulation. In order to develop 5G link- and system-level simulators, it is
necessary to identify and understand all key characteristics of DECT technology.
Nevertheless, the mMTC use case offers D2D communications, which made simulation
analysis extremely cumbersome. In this paper, we also explain the different
maneuvers in order to fasten the simulations and reduce complexity while
respecting all DECT specifications. Simulation results provided from link- and
system-level simulations for both URLLC and mMTC use cases show that DECT-2020
component RIT meets the IMT-2020 requirements.

NTRODUCTION The International Telecommunication Union Radiocommunication sector
(ITU-R) has set the requirements for International Mobile
Telecommunications-2020 (IMT-2020 5G Standard). With the rise of new wireless
communications, artificial intelligence, and big data, several enabling radio
technologies standardized by different standardization organisms such as 3GPP
and ETSI have been proposed within the ITU-R Working Party 5D. The European
telecommunications standards Institute (ETSI)-digital enhanced cordless
telecommunications (DECT) Forum was submitted to ITU-R; claiming to have met the
minimum technology requirements that need to be fulfilled in order to pass [1].
Independent evaluation groups (IEG) participated in evaluating DECT submission
involving Ultra Reliable Low Latency Communications (URLLC) and massive Machine
Type Communications (mMTC) and INRS took part of the process within the Canadian
evaluation group (CEG) using guidelines for the evaluation process in [2]. The
DECT evaluation also garnered the attention of the research community and led to
several evaluation publications [3]–[6]. The DECT-2020 NR offers star and mesh

Contributions and implications:
The work contributes practical insight for DECT-2020 NR and related next-generation wireless systems. It provides experimental or simulated evidence for the proposed scheme and highlights how the results support stricter reliability, lower latency, or improved resource utilization in private network or industrial IoT scenarios.
