Title: ITG-Fachbericht 319: Mobilkommunikation, 20. – 21.05.2025 in Osnabrück

Summary:

This paper addresses the central research challenge of the selected DECT-2020 NR / 5G / wireless networking domain. It focuses on how the proposed design or algorithm improves reliability, coverage, resource management, or performance for emerging URLLC, mMTC, or mesh-networked applications.

Problem and motivation:
—Low Power Wide Area Networks (LPWAN) enable energy-efﬁcient communication over
long distances, making them interesting for Internet of Things (IoT)
applications. Some applications require a higher data rate than standard LPWAN
while keeping a moderate range for keeping infrastructure requirements low. This
paper provides a comparative evaluation of two LPWAN technologies, LoRa
operating at 2.4 GHz and DECT NR+ for these kind of applications. We conducted
modeling and experimental evaluations to assess their performance in terms of
range and data rate across indoor, indoor-outdoor, and outdoor scenarios. For
modeling we applied the log-distance path loss model, accounting for
environmental conditions. Results show that LoRa achieves signiﬁcantly greater
range, with up to 259 meters in indoor-outdoor scenarios, compared to DECT NR+
with a maximum of 16 meters. Outdoor evaluations revealed LoRa’s capability to
maintain connectivity at distances exceeding 1 kilometer in non-line-of-sight
conditions. However, DECT NR+ offers higher data rates, reaching up to 3744
kBit/s, making it advantageous for applications demanding robust data rates in
limited-range scenarios. The ﬁndings highlight the trade-offs between range and
data rate.

NR+, ISM, RF Modeling I. I NTRODUCTION The Internet of Things (IoT) is rapidly
evolving, transforming the way we live and work. Modern devices are increasingly
equipped with wireless radio interfaces, enabling seamless communication. In the
context of smart city projects and industrial applications, the number of
wireless sensors operating within the 868 MHz band has signiﬁcantly grown in the
last years. This trend demonstrates the importance of Low Power Wide Area
Networks (LPWANs) for IoT solutions, with LoRaWAN being a prime solution for
energy efﬁciency and extended range. LoRaWAN in the 868 MHz band offers key
advantages, including excellent building penetration, long battery life, and
extended range [1] [2]. These characteristics are particularly crucial for IoT
applications like water meter readings [3]. However, its low data rate and
regulatory restrictions, such as the 1% duty cycle limitation in Europe, pose
challenges to dynamic control of connected actuators [4]. Such constraints limit
its potential for real-time applications

Contributions and implications:
The work contributes practical insight for DECT-2020 NR and related next-generation wireless systems. It provides experimental or simulated evidence for the proposed scheme and highlights how the results support stricter reliability, lower latency, or improved resource utilization in private network or industrial IoT scenarios.
