Title: Empowering Near-URLLC IoT with 5G DECT2020 NR: Current State and the Road Ahead

Summary:

This paper addresses the central research challenge of the selected DECT-2020 NR / 5G / wireless networking domain. It focuses on how the proposed design or algorithm improves reliability, coverage, resource management, or performance for emerging URLLC, mMTC, or mesh-networked applications.

Problem and motivation:
DECT-2020 is a standardized multi-hop technology for 5G massive machine-type
communications (mMTC). By relying on multi-hop communications, DECT-2020 allows
for cost-controlled flexible deployments that enable a plethora of new services,
whose requirements in terms of latency, reliability, and throughput are
difficult to satisfy by utilizing cellular Internet-ofThings technologies
(CIoT). In this article, we outline new DECT-2020 use-cases ranging from mMTC to
ultra-reliable low-latency communications (URLLC), showing that many modern
services lie between these two extremes. We utilize system level simulations to
demonstrate how these “near-URLLC” use-cases are satisfied by the DECT-2020. We
show that the current evolution of DECT-2020 can provide near-URLLC operation
when no multi-hop communications are required, even under interference from
competing URLLC/ mMTC traffic. However, provisioning of nearURLLC services over
multiple hops requires additional enhancements.

Approach and methods:
The main services specified by ITU-R and 3GPP for 5G systems are enhanced mobile
broadband (eMBB), massive machine type communications (mMTC), and ultra-reliable
low-latency communications (URLLC). The eMBB service has already been supported
by the current evolution of 3GPP 5G New Radio (NR) systems. The mMTC is expected
to be delivered via cellular Internet of Things (CIoT) solutions such as LTE-M
enhancements for machine type communications (eMTC) and Narrowband Internet-of-
Things (NB-IoT), as well as unlicensed band solutions, such as DECT2020.
However, the support for URLLC IoT services lags behind. DECT-2020 NR is a 5G
technology developed for mMTC and URLLC operating in the license-exempt and
licensed bands below 6 GHz in local self-hosted deployments [1]. Recent studies
[2, 3] demonstrated that it can support up to multiple millions of radio devices

Contributions and implications:
The work contributes practical insight for DECT-2020 NR and related next-generation wireless systems. It provides experimental or simulated evidence for the proposed scheme and highlights how the results support stricter reliability, lower latency, or improved resource utilization in private network or industrial IoT scenarios.
