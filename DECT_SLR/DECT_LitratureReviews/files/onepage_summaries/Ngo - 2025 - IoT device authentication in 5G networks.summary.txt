Summary:

This paper addresses the central research challenge of the selected DECT-2020 NR / 5G / wireless networking domain. It focuses on how the proposed design or algorithm improves reliability, coverage, resource management, or performance for emerging URLLC, mMTC, or mesh-networked applications.

Problem and motivation:
The rapid expansion of the Internet of Things and Industrial IoT has accelerated
the adoption of distributed sensing, automation, and data-driven decision-making
across critical sectors such as manufacturing, energy, healthcare, and
logistics. IoT devices typically possess limited computational resources,
heterogeneous hardware architectures, and diverse communication protocols,
making secure and scalable integration into modern communication infrastructures
a persistent challenge. Although 5G provides high reliability, ultra-low
latency, and massive connectivity—factors which makes it a promising foundation
for IoT systems—current integration approaches often rely on complex
architectures, proprietary gateways, and heavyweight security frameworks that
limit their practicality in resource-constrained and industrial environments. To
address these limitations, this thesis presents a lightweight and interoperable
IoTto-5G integration framework featuring a minimal User Equipment and gNB
gateway stack implemented in C. The system can operate directly on embedded IoT
devices such as Raspberry Pi and non-cellular IIoT platforms like DECT-2020 NR,
e.g. Nordic Semiconductor nRF91 family. Moreover, the UE and gNB gateways are
designed to connect seamlessly with the private 5G Core Networks for device
authentication, enabling secure, scalable, and standards-compliant integration
across heterogeneous IoT environments. Experimental validation demonstrates that
the designed UE and gNB gateway interoperate seamlessly with both commercial and
open-source 5G systems, including Cumucore, OpenAirInterface, and free5GC.
Unlike traditional IoT gateways that locally handle protocol translation and
security overhead, the proposed design offloads authentication, session
management, and traffic control directly to the 5GC, thereby reducing the
computational burden on edge IoT and IIoT devices while ensuring full compliance
with 3GPP standards.
