Summary:

This paper addresses the central research challenge of the selected DECT-2020 NR / 5G / wireless networking domain. It focuses on how the proposed design or algorithm improves reliability, coverage, resource management, or performance for emerging URLLC, mMTC, or mesh-networked applications.

Problem and motivation:
Rural remote areas have unique challenges for providing reliable and high-speed
cellular connectivity, which makes them unattractive for mobile network
operators. The Horizon Europe COMMECT project has addressed connectivity in
rural remote areas by 5G private network deployments, which so far have mainly
been studied and deployed in industrial environment. The evaluation of the 5G
private network deployments is conducted through field trials in four different
environment, each of them characterized by different challenges and aiming to
support different agro-forestry use cases. In particular, the evaluations relate
to 1) indoor environments (e.g. hospitals and educational centers), 2) forestry,
3) livestock transportation, and 4) mines. The obtained results showcase that 5G
private networks can support the proposed use cases. For example, in indoor
environments, a downlink and uplink throughput of 700 Mbps and 50 Mbps,
respectively, were measured, which are sufficient to enable e.g. remote
monitoring of hospital patients. Moreover, it was measured that at least 10 Mbps
in the uplink can be achieved for up to 300 m deep in the forest. The 10 Mbps
target is also met in the farm location, including a satellite backhaul, which
can enable e.g. the use case of (un)loading livestock from/to the truck.
Additionally, throughput of more than 100 Mbps was measured at the mine
location, which is sufficient to enhance safety by e.g. operating autonomous
vehicles.

Key findings:
AND FUTURE WORK 5G private networks have gained a lot of attention for
deployments in industrial environments. Within the COMMECT project, different 5G
private network deployments have been proposed to address the connectivity
challenges in remote areas and enable new use cases. In particular, use cases
within four different environments have been considered, namely indoor
environments (for e.g. healthcare or educational centers), forestry, livestock
transportation, and mines. The proposed network deployments are diverse in terms
of hardware (and thus cost) and configuration, and they have been evaluated via
field trials. The results have shown that the deployed networks can support the
presented use cases and that there is a clear trade-off between performance and
cost. This highlights the need for designing 5G private networks based on the
use case and the respective requirements, which could potentially lead to new
business models and opportunities within the telecom sector. Therefore, this
work has performed an empirical assessment and validation across challenging and
underexplored remote scenarios, which are essential for understanding the
feasibility and limitations of 5G private networks in rural contexts. For future
work, we propose to study the performance of other technologies which could
potentially support the use cases, e.g. Wi-Fi.

Contributions and implications:
The work contributes practical insight for DECT-2020 NR and related next-generation wireless systems. It provides experimental or simulated evidence for the proposed scheme and highlights how the results support stricter reliability, lower latency, or improved resource utilization in private network or industrial IoT scenarios.
